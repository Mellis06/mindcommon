# Data governance principles

This document is a starting point for co-design. It is not a legal opinion, ethics approval, privacy notice, or substitute for community agreement.

## Purpose limitation

Every proposed data use should have a defined purpose, responsible organisation, scope, retention period, and review pathway. A broad statement such as "future research" is not an adequate design substitute for explaining foreseeable uses.

## Consent and choice

A future consent record should capture at least:

- participant-facing notice identifier and version;
- purpose and scope of the permission;
- affirmative choice and timestamp;
- method and context of collection;
- current status and any withdrawal;
- language/accessibility format presented;
- responsible controller or programme;
- applicable expiry or review trigger.

Consent must be understandable, voluntary, specific to the relevant use, and supported by meaningful alternatives where possible. The system must not imply that consent alone resolves every legal or ethical obligation.

## Withdrawal

A withdrawal should block future processing that relies on the withdrawn permission, subject to review of applicable legal obligations and clearly explained exceptions. The system should preserve a minimal record needed to demonstrate the change and accountability, without retaining unnecessary sensitive content.

## Access governance

Access decisions should be purpose- and scope-bound, time-limited, reviewed by authorised people, and subject to appeal. Separate request approval from technical enforcement. The enforcement layer must verify the current approval and consent/policy state every time.

## Community governance

The project should co-design how affected communities participate in setting policy, selecting representatives, reviewing new uses, challenging decisions, and receiving benefits. Representation, conflicts of interest, quorum, decision publication, and appeal mechanisms need explicit treatment.

## Minimisation and retention

Collect the minimum information needed for an agreed purpose. Establish retention and deletion schedules before collecting real information. Do not collect detailed narratives or identifiers merely because the software can store them.

## Incident accountability

A future operating model should define incident reporting, response roles, notification decisions, support for affected people, evidence preservation, and post-incident community accountability.
